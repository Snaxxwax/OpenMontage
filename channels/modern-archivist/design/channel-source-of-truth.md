# Modern Archivist / Failure Ledger Channel Source of Truth

Status: canonical channel development brief

## Executive decision

Build Corporate True Crime, not a chart/document explainer channel.

The channel identity is:

> Corporate True Crime: cinematic investigations into companies, founders, products, platforms, banks, scams, and financial machines that collapsed, lied, hurt people, or quietly rewrote history.

The show identity is:

> Failure Ledger by Modern Archivist.

The production promise is:

> Every episode reconstructs how a company, product, founder, bank, platform, or financial machine broke, using public evidence, source footage, digital artifacts, reconstructed moments, and cinematic investigation boards.

## Non-negotiable visual rule

Documents, charts, filings, and graphs are evidence. They are not the show.

Use them as short receipt moments, contradiction reveals, or source-backed anchors. Do not build long sequences out of static SEC filings, court PDFs, tables, generic line charts, or narration-over-slide visuals.

Every document must become a scene. If it cannot become a scene, it stays in research.

## Why this channel wins

1. Monetization upside

The channel sits at the overlap of finance, business, legal, technology, SaaS, cybersecurity, and investing. These are generally high-value advertiser categories. Public third-party benchmarks consistently place finance/legal/business/tech above generic entertainment, but YouTube does not publish official niche CPM/RPM guarantees.

2. Broad viewer psychology

Corporate True Crime borrows true-crime mechanics without becoming generic crime content:

- betrayal
- mystery
- greed
- hidden warning signs
- public contradiction
- consequences for customers, employees, investors, or users
- a final answer to: how did the machine break?

3. Structured evidence feasibility

The channel can be grounded in public and semi-structured sources:

- SEC EDGAR: filings, 8-Ks, 10-Ks, 10-Qs, S-1s, DEF 14A, XBRL facts
- CourtListener / RECAP: complaints, dockets, filings, opinions, orders, enforcement actions
- Wayback CDX: deleted claims, archived landing pages, old marketing, scrubbed statements
- Wikipedia / Wikidata: discovery and entity resolution only; not final proof for risky claims
- GitHub / public status pages: incidents, commits, code artifacts, outages, postmortems
- source footage: hearings, interviews, keynotes, product demos, ads, launch material, public statements where legally usable or recreatable

## Topic selection rule

Greenlight a topic only if it has at least three of these five:

1. Big quantified stakes: money lost, valuation destroyed, customers affected, jobs lost, users harmed, or infrastructure damaged.
2. Clear antagonist or failure mechanism: founder, board, incentive, bug, leverage, regulatory gap, governance failure, or market structure.
3. Visual artifacts available: source footage, archived pages, product UI, public demos, ads, interviews, hearings, screenshots, social posts.
4. Public evidence trail: SEC, court docs, archived web, official reports, status pages, GitHub, transcripts, credible reporting.
5. Emotional consequence: depositors, customers, employees, patients, creators, small businesses, communities, or investors.

Reject or park a topic if it only has filings and charts.

## Preferred pilot topics

Best proof-of-format candidates:

1. Nikola

Working title: The Fake Truck That Became a $30 Billion Company

Why: big dollar number, obvious visual artifact, founder character, SEC/legal evidence, product-demo contradiction, general-audience clarity, and less dependence on boring charts.

2. MoviePass

Why: recognizable consumer product, app/UI visuals, funny/tragic collapse mechanics, strong product-failure framing.

3. WeWork

Why: abundant founder footage, office visuals, S-1 material, huge valuation collapse, clear character-driven arc.

4. SVB

Why: already in progress and highly monetizable, but visually harder because the core mechanism pushes toward balance sheets, bonds, and charts. Use only after the source-footage/artifact pipeline can make financial mechanisms cinematic.

5. FTX / Theranos / Boeing

Why: proven demand and strong evidence, but crowded or legally/sensitively complex. Use after the format is proven.

## Episode structure

Target duration:

- Standard: 10-14 minutes
- Major stories with abundant source material: 18-25 minutes

Default structure:

1. Cold open, 0:00-0:20
   Show the collapse or contradiction first. No intro animation, no channel greeting, no throat-clearing.

2. Promise, 0:20-0:45
   State the mystery and the failure mechanism the episode will solve.

3. The machine, 0:45-2:30
   Explain how the company/product/bank/platform was supposed to work using recreated UI, source footage, artifact boards, and puppet framing.

4. The first crack, 2:30-4:30
   Introduce the first dated warning sign.

5. The paper trail, 4:30-7:30
   Use evidence, but visually translate filings/dockets into scenes, contradictions, source montages, archived-page diffs, and artifact trails.

6. The decision that broke it, 7:30-10:30
   Isolate the core mechanism: duration mismatch, fake demo, leverage, fraud, bad incentive, governance failure, or technical flaw.

7. The collapse, 10:30-12:30
   Timeline accelerates. Use news-style source montage, social posts, status pages, shutdown pages, legal filings, and customer consequences.

8. The verdict, 12:30-14:00
   The Modern Archivist closes the ledger: what failed, who paid, and what the machine was actually optimized for.

## Visual hierarchy

1. Source footage and public video

Use interviews, hearings, product demos, launch videos, ads, keynotes, creator footage, conference clips, and public statements when rights/provenance allow. When not usable directly, recreate the relevant artifact or summarize with a labeled editorial visual.

2. Recreated digital artifacts

High-priority visual language:

- archived websites
- product pages
- bank dashboards
- investor portals
- app screens
- emails
- Slack/Discord/message threads
- founder tweet timelines
- GitHub commits
- status pages
- customer complaint feeds
- press-release pages
- app-store pages

3. Cinematic investigation board

Modern digital case board, not a literal corkboard:

- timeline spine
- evidence cards
- logos/faces as actors
- money-flow paths
- warning-sign pins
- before/after website diffs
- deleted-claim overlays
- consequence maps

4. Full-body Modern Archivist puppet

The Archivist is the recurring investigator/anchor:

- opens the case
- returns at act breaks
- points to evidence
- walks through the case room
- reacts to contradictions
- closes with the verdict

Preserve a consistent full-body puppet rig. Do not use floating-head, portrait-only, or partial puppet variants unless explicitly approved.

5. Motion language

Use motion to create case-building, not decoration:

- parallax evidence walls
- camera pushes into recreated UI
- kinetic quote reveals
- contradiction overlays
- before/after archived-page wipes
- money-flow trails
- timeline chapter cards
- source-footage frames moving through a case board
- dossier assembly sequences

## Runtime strategy

Remotion remains the canonical final renderer for:

- full-body Archivist puppet
- narration sync
- deterministic React state
- channel frame
- final episode assembly
- render QC and smoke tests

HyperFrames is a first-class optional runtime for:

- source-rich montage segments
- kinetic typography
- GSAP-heavy transitions
- website-to-video sequences
- case-board motion wrappers
- UI/product motion packages

Do not silently swap runtimes. At proposal time, present Remotion and HyperFrames if both are available, recommend one, and record the runtime selection.

Preferred near-term architecture: HyperFrames can generate local motion-rich segment assets; Remotion composes those assets with the puppet-led channel frame.

## Evidence rules

Every factual claim must be categorized:

- primary evidence
- secondary reporting
- inference
- allegation
- admission
- finding
- settlement
- conviction
- dismissal
- illustrative only

Every evidence-backed claim should retain:

- source URL or document reference
- retrieval date
- document/publication date
- quote span or evidence excerpt where practical
- confidence level
- legal status

Do not treat allegations as proven facts. Do not use Wikipedia as final proof for misconduct claims; use it for discovery and entity mapping.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- research-deck visuals
- long static receipts
- generic stock montage with no evidence relationship
- endless SEC filing screenshots
- highlighted court PDFs as the main visual surface
- generic line charts or KPI grids unless they reveal a story turn
- permanent bottom-right mascot
- unlabeled illustrative material
- image-to-video puppet workarounds
- live network fetches during render
- visual choices that weaken source provenance

## Pipeline implications

The pipeline must become source-footage/artifact-first.

Add or formalize a content_collection stage before scriptwriting. Its job is to answer:

> What can we actually show?

For each candidate story, collect:

- source footage candidates
- public video/interview/hearing/keynote/demo candidates
- archived websites and changed claims
- product UI/screenshots
- ads and marketing claims
- social posts
- legal/SEC evidence
- visual metaphors
- puppet interaction opportunities
- copyright/provenance status
- runtime affinity: remotion, hyperframes, or either

The script must be written around available visual evidence, not abstract ideas.

## Local-First Architecture Principles

The Modern Archivist is a local-first but not synthetic-first production system:

- Local generation is used to reduce cost, improve privacy, and create repeatable outputs.
- Local tools are means to an end, not the primary story mechanism.
- Local TTS is acceptable only if it passes voice consistency and disclosure checks.
- ComfyUI and local video diffusion are optional source-asset support only, not the foundation of the episode.
- Real source footage, public artifacts, archived pages, product UI, filings, launch demos, and recreated evidence cards remain the default visual language.
- Remotion remains the canonical final assembler for deterministic, evidence-driven narrative.

### Local Tool Principles

1. Cost and Privacy: Use local tools to reduce per-call API costs and protect iterative work privacy.
2. Determinism: Local generation provides repeatable, controlled outputs.
3. Provenance: Any AI/generated content must be explicitly disclosed and reviewed.
4. Evidence Primacy: Local tools support the evidence chain; they do not replace it.

### AI and Synthetic Content Policy

- Generated assets must not replace real evidence or source material.
- All synthetic or AI-generated content requires:
  - Explicit provenance tracking
  - Disclosure review before publication
  - Clear labeling when used
- Reject content that could mislead viewers about its synthetic nature
- Use generated visuals only when:
  - They support the evidence thesis
  - Cannot be mistaken for real footage
  - Are clearly labeled as recreations

### Tooling Constraints

- Avoid autonomous generation loops
- Require human review for all generated content
- Preserve the editorial intent of evidence-cinema
- Prioritize source material over synthetic reconstruction

Do not pivot the channel into a chained AI video generation system. The goal is evidence-cinema: thesis-driven, source-footage-first, deterministic, and credible.

## Acceptance bar for future development

A development change supports the channel only if it improves at least one of these:

1. Source-footage/artifact collection
2. Evidence provenance and claim safety
3. Cinematic case-building visual modes
4. Full-body puppet consistency
5. Runtime selection clarity and HyperFrames/Remotion handoff
6. Render speed and objective QC
7. Repeatable topic selection and episode franchising
8. Local tool integration without compromising evidence discipline
9. AI/generative technology provenance and disclosure practices

If a change only makes it easier to produce charts, static documents, generic explainer scenes, or unchecked synthetic media, it is not aligned with this source of truth.
