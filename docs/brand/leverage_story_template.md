# Asymmetric Leverage Story Template

Version: 1.0
Last updated: 2026-05-17
Status: Active — official episode structure for all Asymmetric productions

---

## Purpose

Every Asymmetric episode follows this ten-part structure. The structure is not a rigid script outline — it is an evidence-first narrative architecture that forces the leverage mechanism to become visible before the episode ends.

Do not deviate from the structure without a documented reason. The structure exists because it works: it places the receipt before the explanation, the mechanism before the moral, and the takeaway before the call to action.

---

## The Ten-Part Leverage Story

### 1. Consequence

Open with something that already happened. A concrete, verifiable outcome that proves the leverage exists.

Not a question. Not a statistic without stakes. Not institutional background.

A consequence: a termination, a rate increase, an acquisition blocked, a service denied, a bill passed, a market closed.

The consequence must be specific enough that the viewer can look it up. It must be dramatic enough that a scrolling viewer stops.

**Required:** Show the consequence before naming the mechanism that caused it.

**Advertiser-safe language:** "In [year], [entity] [did the thing]. Here is what that cost."

---

### 2. First Receipt

The first piece of primary-source evidence. A document, a filing, a rate case, a contract clause, a court record, a regulatory order.

The receipt must be legible on screen. It must be specific. It must be the kind of thing that was not supposed to be noticed.

**Required:** The receipt appears as a visual — a close-up of the document, not a paraphrase of it.

**Purpose:** Establish that this is a forensic briefing, not an opinion piece. The viewer sees the proof before they hear the argument.

---

### 3. Public Story

The version of events that most people believe. The official explanation. The press release. The consensus narrative.

State it briefly and without mockery. The public story is not stupid — it is what reasonable people believe with limited information. Treat it fairly.

**Purpose:** Establish the gap between appearance and mechanism. The viewer needs to know the surface story before seeing through it.

---

### 4. Hidden Mechanism

How the system actually works beneath the public story. The technical, legal, or structural reality that produces the outcome.

Use diagram language here. This is where the Leverage Map begins to take shape.

**Required:** The mechanism must be provable. Every claim in this section must connect to a receipt (current or upcoming). If it cannot be shown with evidence, it does not go in the mechanism section.

**Language:** Describe the mechanism, not the intent. "The rate structure allows [entity] to recover costs from ratepayers before earning a return on equity" — not "They scam ratepayers."

---

### 5. Control Surface

Name and reveal the specific mechanism that concentrates control. The valve. The gate. The clause. The chokepoint.

This is the most important visual moment in the episode. The control surface must appear on screen as a close-up: a specific section of a contract, a rate schedule line item, a regulatory definition, a platform policy clause.

**Required:** Show the control surface as a visual. Amber overlay on the element that concentrates control.

**The question this section answers:** Where exactly does the power sit?

---

### 6. Dependency

Show who depends on the control surface — and has no alternative.

This is what makes the leverage real. Without dependency, control is academic. With dependency, control is leverage.

Map the dependencies explicitly: which entities, users, ratepayers, operators, or governments have no exit option.

**Language:** "At this point, [dependent entity] has no alternative. Here is why: [structural reason, not moral failure]."

---

### 7. Cost Transfer

Show who absorbs the cost that the controlling entity does not pay.

Cost transfer is the mechanism by which private entities externalize costs onto ratepayers, taxpayers, employees, or downstream businesses.

Name the transfer explicitly. Use receipts where available: rate case filings, subsidy disclosures, public balance sheets, incentive agreements.

**Required language examples:**
- "cost transfer"
- "ratepayer exposure"
- "public-cost exposure"
- "incentive structure"
- "externalized operating cost"

**Avoid:** "They're billing you for their profits." (True in spirit, legally imprecise and reckless.)

---

### 8. Beneficiary

Name who captures the value that the cost transfer creates.

Be specific. Name the entity, the mechanism by which they capture value, and the magnitude if documentable.

Do not editorialize beyond the evidence. "Company X captured $[N] in subsidized construction cost while ratepayers absorbed $[M] in rate increases" — not "They looted the public."

**The question this section answers:** Who wins?

---

### 9. Accountability Gap

Explain why the mechanism escapes consequence.

The accountability gap is structural, not personal. It is the absence of an oversight mechanism, the regulatory capture of an agency, the jurisdictional void, the enforcement gap, the sunsetted disclosure requirement.

Do not imply that individuals are corrupt unless you have documentary proof. The mechanism is sufficient.

**The question this section answers:** Why hasn't this been fixed?

**Language examples:**
- "No public accounting mechanism requires [entity] to disclose [cost]."
- "The regulatory agency responsible for [oversight] has not [enforced / reviewed / updated] since [year]."
- "Disclosure is required at the state level, but not aggregated federally."

---

### 10. Leverage Takeaway

The reusable operator-level mental model. One clear idea the viewer can carry out of the video and apply to adjacent systems.

Not a summary. Not a conclusion. Not "so next time you see this, think about it."

A framework: "When you see [signal], that means [mechanism is operating]. The question to ask is [specific question]. The document to find is [specific document type]."

**Required elements:**
- What to watch for in adjacent systems
- What questions to ask
- What documents to find (if applicable)
- Safe action language — informational, lawful, non-partisan, non-harassing

The leverage takeaway is the reason viewers share the video. It gives them a new way to see a class of situations, not just knowledge about this one case.

---

## Required Episode Questions

Every episode must answer all eight of these questions — explicitly, not implicitly:

1. Where is the leverage?
2. What is the control surface?
3. What receipt proves it?
4. Who depends on it?
5. Who pays?
6. Who benefits?
7. Why is accountability weak?
8. What can the viewer do with this knowledge?

If any of these questions cannot be answered with evidence, the episode is not ready to script.

---

## Advertiser-Safe Language Patterns

The episode template relies on this language discipline throughout. See `docs/brand/advertiser_safety.md` for the complete reference.

Summary:
- Criticize the mechanism, not the person
- Name the incentive structure, not the character
- Show the receipt, do not speculate
- Use cost transfer language, not robbery language
- Keep the tone controlled — severity is in the evidence, not the adjectives

## Leverage Brief Gate

Before scripting any episode, complete `leverage_brief.json` and have it approved.

The leverage brief locks:
- The hidden mechanism
- The control surface
- The cost transfer chain
- The beneficiaries
- The accountability gap
- The viewer takeaway
- The advertiser safety posture

No script begins without an approved leverage brief. The brief is the intellectual contract for the episode.
