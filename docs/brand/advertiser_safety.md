# Asymmetric Advertiser Safety

Version: 1.0
Last updated: 2026-05-17
Status: Active — applies to all scripts, narration, titles, thumbnails and descriptions

---

## Core Rule

**Attack the mechanism, not the person.**
**Show the receipt.**
**Name the incentive.**
**Avoid reckless accusations.**
**Keep the tone controlled.**

Asymmetric is a forensic brand. Its authority comes from evidence and structure, not from outrage. Outrage content is not harder-hitting — it is weaker, because it trades precision for emotion. Precision is the brand.

---

## Approved Language

Use these terms to describe mechanisms, costs, and incentives:

| Term | Use When |
|---|---|
| `cost transfer` | A cost borne by one party is shifted to another through structural means |
| `incentive structure` | The system of rewards that shapes behavior regardless of stated intent |
| `ratepayer exposure` | Utility ratepayers absorb costs through regulated rate increases |
| `public-cost exposure` | Taxpayers or public entities absorb costs through subsidies, infrastructure, or externalities |
| `control surface` | The specific mechanism or clause through which control is exercised |
| `dependency` | An entity has no structural alternative to the controlling party |
| `market power` | A party's ability to influence prices or terms due to structural position |
| `accountability gap` | The absence of an oversight mechanism that would normally constrain the behavior |
| `regulatory gap` | A jurisdictional void, enforcement lapse, or outdated rule that permits the mechanism |
| `chokepoint` | A point in a system where control concentrates due to structural scarcity |
| `extraction layer` | A structural fee, toll, or markup built into the system |
| `externalized cost` | A cost absorbed by parties outside the transaction |
| `incentive misalignment` | The interests of the controlling party differ from those of the dependent party |

---

## Avoid This Language

These terms are imprecise, legally risky, or damage advertiser trust:

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| `robbery` / `stealing` | Implies criminal intent without evidentiary basis |
| `scam` | Implies fraud — a legal finding, not an editorial one |
| `criminals` / `criminal behavior` | Requires a conviction or documented charge |
| `parasites` | Dehumanizing — damages brand trust and advertiser confidence |
| `traitors` | Political framing with no analytical content |
| `corrupt elites` | Vague, inflammatory, non-specific |
| `sold us out` | Implies betrayal of a trust relationship without proof |
| `they're stealing from you` | Conflates cost transfer with theft |
| `they're profiting off your misery` | Emotional framing that cannot be substantiated as written |
| `the [group] cabal` | Implies conspiracy without evidence |
| rage-bait modifiers generally | Reduce the brand to clickbait and alienate the primary audience |

## Person vs. Mechanism

Asymmetric criticizes **mechanisms**, **incentive structures**, **control surfaces**, and **cost transfers** — not individual people, companies as moral actors, or broad demographic groups.

### Acceptable

- "The rate structure allows [company] to recover construction costs from ratepayers before the plant produces power."
- "The incentive agreement shields [company] from property tax for 20 years, transferring that cost to other local taxpayers."
- "The platform policy gives [company] sole discretion to terminate accounts with no appeal mechanism."

### Not Acceptable

- "[CEO name] personally decided to rob ratepayers."
- "[Company] is a criminal operation."
- "Big [industry] is destroying the country."

### The Test

Read the claim aloud. Ask: "Could a reasonable person call this defamatory?" If yes, rewrite using mechanism language. The mechanism is always sufficient — you do not need the inflammatory framing.

## Title Defensibility Test

Every title must pass this test before production:

1. **Is it literally true?** If not, rewrite it.
2. **Could a named entity claim defamation?** If yes, rephrase using mechanism or system language.
3. **Does it imply a criminal act without a documented charge or conviction?** If yes, reframe as structural or incentive-based.
4. **Is it inflammatory enough to trigger a demonetization review?** Consider whether the framing can achieve the same impact with controlled language.

### Strong Titles (Pass)
- "You Pay for the AI Boom Three Times"
- "The Company That Can Cut Off Your Business Overnight"
- "The Data Center Deal Hidden in Your Power Bill"
- "The Rule That Controls Every App on Your Phone"
- "Where the Leverage Is in Cloud Infrastructure"

### Risky Titles (Rewrite)
- "Big Tech Is Robbing You" → "You Pay for Big Tech's AI Buildout Three Times"
- "The Corrupt Scam Behind AI" → "The Hidden Cost Structure Behind the AI Buildout"
- "Corporate Criminals Are Looting America" → "How the AI Infrastructure Boom Transfers Costs to the Public"

## Thumbnail Advertiser Safety

Thumbnails must not:
- Depict real individuals in misleading or defamatory contexts
- Use shock imagery unrelated to the mechanism
- Imply criminal conduct not documented in the episode
- Use dehumanizing imagery for any group

Thumbnails should:
- Use Asymmetric visual archetypes: role proxies (Gatekeeper, Extractor, Broker) instead of real faces
- Show control surfaces, systems, documents — not people in unflattering contexts
- Convey the mechanism, not the outrage

## Risk Level Classification

Every `leverage_brief.json` must declare an `advertiser_safety_posture.risk_level`:

| Level | Description | Required Action |
|---|---|---|
| `low` | Pure structural analysis — no named individuals, no implied criminal conduct | Proceed |
| `medium` | Named entities criticized for documented behavior; no criminal implication | Legal review of specific claims before publish |
| `high` | Named individuals, implied misconduct, or politically charged subject | Full legal review required; consider mechanism reframe |

High-risk episodes are not prohibited. They require additional review and may require reframing the hook or title.

---

## The Trust Rule

The channel's authority is its trust with the primary audience. That audience is skeptical, smart, and will immediately detect when a claim exceeds the evidence.

Overstating a claim loses a viewer permanently. Understating a claim but showing a devastating receipt keeps them.

Always choose the receipt over the hyperbole. The receipt is more credible and more damaging to the mechanism you are exposing than any adjective.
